# Designing Cinematic Storytelling

## Core Principle

Design around what the audience knows, when they learn it, and whose experience they share. Every shot and cut must clarify story, space, or emotion.

## When Not to Use

Skip it for single-shot pieces, pure kinetic typography, data visualization, or motion graphics without spatial or narrative continuity.

## Required Inputs

- Scene or beat summary; focal character, conflict, and emotional turn.
- Location, time, characters, props, movement, runtime, and production limits.
- Information to reveal, delay, or hide.

## Process

1. **State the scene contract:** focal character, conflict, emotional turn, reveal order, and final image.
2. **Build beats:** dialogue, action, reaction, look, prop, entrance, exit, and time change. Give each beat one viewer task: geography, action, emotion, clue, reaction, or transition.
3. **Lock space:** action axis, positions, eyelines, movement directions, landmarks, appearance, wardrobe, props, and handedness.
4. **Design functional coverage:** master when geography matters; relationship views, POV/near-POV, reactions, and inserts. Add fallback coverage when one failed long take would collapse the scene.
5. **Specify shots from purpose:** size, angle, lens character, focus, blocking, movement, and one dominant center of interest.
6. **Motivate cuts:** use action, eyeline, sound, reaction, entrance, exit, reveal, or a need for new information.
7. **Protect continuity and contrast:** preserve eyelines, direction, landmarks, and matched action; vary adjacent shots enough to avoid accidental jumps.
8. **Choose transitions by meaning:** default to hard cuts in continuous action; use sound bridges, wipes, dissolves, or fades only for a named time or emotional relationship.
9. **Test silently:** confirm the event, focal character, space, and emotional change remain legible without sound.

## Output Contract

Produce a scene contract, beat sheet, functional shot list, spatial/continuity locks, coverage and fallback plan, transition/sound notes, risks, and self-review. Each shot records purpose, framing, POV, lens/focus, blocking, movement, and cut motivation.

## Minimal Example

For a girl discovering a wolf, `girl -> wolf -> reaction` creates shared discovery. `wolf -> unaware girl` gives the audience superior knowledge and creates suspense.

## Self-Evaluation

Score 1-5; revise anything below 3: viewer knowledge, POV, spatial clarity, shot purpose, editability, cut motivation, and silent-view legibility.

## Common Failures

- One wide shot covers every beat; shots precede POV and reveal decisions.
- Camera movement decorates; cuts join nearly identical views.
- Close-ups erase geography; eyelines or screen direction flip silently.
- Dissolves hide weak contrast; a long take has no fallback coverage.
