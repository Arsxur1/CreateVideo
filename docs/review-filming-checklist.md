# Filming checklist for product reviews

Better footage is the biggest quality lever. Editing cannot show a feature that was never filmed.

## Before you start

- Film vertical (9:16), 4K if the phone allows. 4K lets the edit crop close-ups later.
- Clean the product and the table. Plain background, no clutter.
- Daylight from a window, or one soft lamp. No overhead tube light straight down.
- Lock focus and exposure on the product (long-press on most phones).

## Shots to get (5 seconds each, hold still)

1. **Box**: front of the box, top-down. Then the box opening.
2. **Everything in the box**: laid out, one slow move across it.
3. **Hero close-up**: the product alone, slowly turning or a slow push in.
4. **Each feature, used by a hand**: one clip per feature. A finger pressing the button, a knob
   turning, a mode changing, the port being plugged in. This is what the review talks about,
   so it must be on screen.
5. **The result**: what the product does, in the real place (the room, the desk, the wall).
   Wide first, then closer.
6. **Before and after** of any fix or tweak you made (e.g. the thread on the knob).
7. **Size reference**: the product next to a hand or a common object.

## For night or light products (projectors, lamps)

- Turn every other light off. Lower exposure until the projection is not blown out.
- Film one clip with the lamp switching on in the dark: it makes a strong first second.
- Also take a still photo of each projection. Photos show sharpness better than video.

## Sound

- The camera audio is usually not used. If you want your own voice, record it separately in a
  quiet room, phone 20 cm from your mouth, one line per take.

## Keep

- Hold every shot for at least 5 seconds, even if you only need 2.
- Name the files by what they show: `button-press.mp4`, `knob-focus.mp4`.
